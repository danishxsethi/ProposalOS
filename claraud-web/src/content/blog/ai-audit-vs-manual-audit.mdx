# Treat an automated finding as a lead to investigate

An automated website check can help organize observations. Its output still needs a person to confirm that the page, date, device, and source are relevant to the business being reviewed.

## Check the evidence

For each proposed finding, record the affected URL, the time it was checked, the tool or source used, and the observation that supports it. Keep the observation separate from an explanation of why it may matter.

If a page could not be reached, a source returned incomplete data, or a check was simulated, say so. Do not describe missing evidence as a confirmed business problem.

## Separate observation from prediction

“The contact link returned an error during this check” is an observation that can be reproduced. “This error costs the business a particular amount of revenue” requires business-specific traffic, conversion, and value data. Without those inputs, leave out the financial prediction.

## Make recommendations reviewable

State the proposed change, who would make it, what access it requires, and how the result would be checked. Avoid promising rankings, leads, revenue, or a delivery date before the scope and baseline are agreed.

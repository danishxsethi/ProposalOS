# Start with a reproducible observation

A slow page, broken form, or hard-to-use mobile layout may be worth fixing. The business impact depends on the site, its visitors, and the task they came to complete. A website check alone cannot establish how much revenue an issue caused.

## Record the conditions

Note the page URL, device class, browser, network conditions, date, and measurement tool. Review important pages more than once and preserve the result so another person can repeat the check.

For performance work, distinguish a lab measurement from field data collected from actual visitors. For conversion work, agree on the baseline and measurement window before attributing a change to a fix.

## Inspect the visitor task

Try the main actions a visitor needs to complete: find a service, understand the service area, call, submit a form, or request an appointment. Record exactly where the journey fails. Do not infer that visitors abandoned without analytics or other supporting evidence.

## Prioritize changes that can be verified

Describe a small change, its owner, and the check that would show whether it worked. A technical improvement is not proof of a ranking increase or a revenue gain. Report those outcomes only when the business has suitable before-and-after data.
